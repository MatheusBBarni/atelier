# ADR-001: Reframe as a governance spine consumed by the sibling packets

## Status

Accepted

## Date

2026-06-15

## Context

The idea began as "upfront plan preview + checkpoint approval," with the plan preview as the V1 lead. Research and a 5-advisor council overturned that framing on three grounds:

1. **Plan preview before execution is table stakes** (Claude Code plan mode, Cline Plan/Act, Devin, Copilot), and a 248-person study (arXiv 2502.01390) found showing users a plan did **not** improve calibrated trust and sometimes hurt it (rubber-stamping plausible-but-flawed plans). The plan card is the commoditized, possibly-trust-negative part.
2. **The plan card binds and previews nothing real** today: `OrchestratorDecision.plan` is dead code (`Vec<String>`, zero readers); the orchestrator routes one step at a time, and execution is explicitly non-binding.
3. **Most of the proposed value already lives in two sibling packets.** `approval-trust-list` ships a fail-closed floor that re-prompts irreversible/catastrophic actions even in `yolo` (the hard gate); `subtask-dag-execution` F4 ships whole-plan structure-approval over committed, fence-enforced nodes (real plan approval + write-scope envelope). The council found the envelope is "only as trustworthy as the fence that enforces it," which exists in DAG but not in an unfenced plan-preview.

The genuinely net-new, unserved sliver was small (a single-agent turn-1 early-abort glance). The product owner chose the **governance-spine** direction over shipping that sliver in isolation or proceeding with the redundant original.

## Decision

Reframe this packet as a **governance spine**: a harness-owned, reusable governance primitive that the two sibling packets consume instead of each building its own approval surface. The spine owns:

- A **shared plan/decision IR** (`Plan { nodes: [PlanNode { id, agent, instruction, write_scope, risk }], edges }`) — the single serializable model the DAG executor enriches with real edges and that scope-reasoning consumes.
- A **reusable governance-checkpoint primitive** — a `PendingGovernanceDecision` riding the proven clarification pause/resume (`RunDriveContext` capture + `drive_and_replay`) and key-routing, with a structured Accept/Reject decision recorded as events and projected to chat.
- The **deterministic risk classification + complexity-gating policy** (classify from the `ActionRequest`/write-scope, never the model's self-graded risk; the editorial "when to interrupt").
- The **hard irreversible-action gate contract** (fires even in `yolo`, fail-closed) — the enforcement contract `approval-trust-list` implements.

This packet's own visible feature is the **turn-1 early-abort "intent echo"** for single-agent runs — the simplest consumer of the spine. `subtask-dag-execution` F4 (whole-plan approval) and `approval-trust-list` (fail-closed floor) become consumers of the same primitive.

## Alternatives Considered

### Alternative 1: Narrow sliver only

- **Description**: Ship just the single-agent turn-1 early-abort intent echo; defer everything else to the siblings.
- **Pros**: Cheap, non-redundant, fast.
- **Cons**: Small; leaves three packets each inventing their own approval/plan model and diverging.
- **Why rejected**: Misses the chance to unify; the owner wanted one coherent governance layer.

### Alternative 2: Keep the original (plan-preview-leads)

- **Description**: Build the L-sized plan-preview-leads idea as first written.
- **Pros**: Matches the initial vision.
- **Cons**: Leads with table stakes the evidence says may not build trust; redundant with both siblings; previews dead-code plans.
- **Why rejected**: Low leverage; the council was unanimous against it.

### Alternative 3: Trust profiles

- **Description**: Per-project/task-type trust postures that govern when any gate fires.
- **Pros**: Leans into the complexity-gating white space as configurable policy.
- **Cons**: Cross-cuts all three packets; a policy layer with no owner for the underlying primitive.
- **Why rejected**: Better expressed as a policy *on top of* the spine, not instead of it; deferred as a possible follow-on.

## Consequences

### Positive

- One event-sourced governance primitive + one plan IR across the harness, instead of three divergent approval UIs.
- Differentiation shifts to the defensible white space (complexity-gating + deterministic risk classification + a non-binding-over-adaptive-executor model) rather than the commoditized plan card.
- The siblings get a tested pause/resume gate and a shared IR "for free."

### Negative

- Requires **re-coordinating two in-flight sibling packets** to depend on this spine — a portfolio/sequencing cost.

### Risks

- **Sequencing/coordination** with `approval-trust-list` and `subtask-dag-execution` — mitigated by defining narrow consumer contracts and shipping the spine + early-abort consumer first.
- **Scope creep** into the siblings' territory — mitigated by an explicit ownership boundary (spine owns the IR + primitive + classification; siblings own the irreversible-action allowlist and the DAG topology respectively).

## Implementation Notes

Reuse the clarification transport (`RunDriveContext` + `drive_and_replay`) but add a distinct `PendingGovernanceDecision` state rather than overloading clarification. Freeze the plan/decision and the Accept/Reject as events for replay determinism. Classify risk once at build time from write-scope/step-count/parallel-groups; never re-infer at render.
